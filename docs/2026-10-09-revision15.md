# Revision 15 — firearm-effect diagnostic counters

The maintainer reports working core VR controls and sword behaviour in the
previous headset run, with firearm effects still incorrect. This revision
instruments the existing muzzle native bridge for targeted follow-up. It does
not adjust weapon recoil strength, haptic intensity, projectile origin,
animation, Niagara positioning or any firing/ownership guards.

## What the bridge records

While an owned firearm lease is ready and enabled, the bridge writes five
`[Boltgun muzzle 3D]` log lines every six seconds, for at most eight windows
(48 seconds). Counters are drained per window and reset on a new firearm lease.
They are diagnostic counts rather than guaranteed numbers of player trigger
pulls: one trigger may spawn multiple projectiles, and some weapon types may
bypass the intercepted projectile helper entirely.

1. **Projectile spawn diagnostic** — `calls` sees the intercepted helper;
   `redirected` counts validated calls with corrected origin.
2. **Shot feedback** — `spawned` and `null` describe validated original-helper
   results; `queued` counts impulses accepted for the visual; `noVisual`
   means the lease has no distinct animated mesh; `capped` means the eight-shot
   pending limit rejected an impulse; `discarded` means a queued impulse was
   revoked before tick; `consumed` counts impulses read by the engine tick.
3. **Visual feedback** — `applied` counts successful recoil offset calls;
   `unavailable` means an eligible visual/state or setter was unavailable;
   `fault` means the UEVR write raised an exception. `restored`,
   `restoreUnavailable`, and `restoreFault` describe attempts to restore the
   exact original visual offset. A successful API call does not prove the
   animation was visible in the headset.
4. **Haptic feedback** — `sent` means the UEVR haptic callback returned;
   `noAPI` means required callbacks are absent; `noSource` means the right
   controller source was unavailable; `throttled` means the 50 ms cooldown
   merged pulses; `fault` indicates an exception and disables later haptics;
   `disabled` counts such later suppressed attempts. The callback returning
   does not guarantee a perceptible PS VR2 Sense vibration.
5. **Niagara flash** — existing `atLocation`, `attached`, `exact`, `moved`,
   `attachedHeld` and `fault` telemetry is unchanged. Attached systems retain
   the original lifetime/parent deliberately.

## Headset follow-up

Test a few individual shots with the Bolter, then one firearm whose recoil or
flash fails, and compare the relevant six-second windows. If `calls=0` while
shooting, this helper was not reached (the cause remains unknown). If
`redirected>0` but `queued=0`, inspect `null`, `noVisual`, and `capped`.
If `queued>0` but `consumed=0`, inspect lease validity or a menu/off transition.
If `consumed>0` but `applied=0`, inspect the visual counters; compare `sent`
with `noSource`, `noAPI` and `throttled` for vibration issues. For flashes,
`attachedHeld>0` identifies a path intentionally left at its native location.

Capture the UEVR log with the weapon name and note which visual/haptic symptom
was observed. No media, logs, assets or dumps should be committed to Git.

Offline tests cover successful and null spawns, pending-cap and no-visual
outcomes, lease revocation, unavailable motion state, haptic callback/source
absence and rate limiting. `verifiedInGame=false` remains until the maintainer
confirms this revision in the headset. Do not deploy this build to the live
profile as part of repository maintenance.
